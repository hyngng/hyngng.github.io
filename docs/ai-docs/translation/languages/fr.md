# French Translation Notes

## Elision and Default-Filling

The impersonal pronoun "on" is a natural way to render a generalized,
unspecified subject in French, but it is not automatically the correct
default. "On" carries a colloquial register; in a more formal essay
passage, "nous" (the writer positioning themselves as part of a shared,
general subject) or an impersonal construction ("il faut que," "il est
nécessaire de," "il arrive que") may better match the register of the
Korean original. Choose based on the formality of the surrounding
paragraph, not habit.

## Verbosity — A Known Failure Mode

French translations of this material have shown a documented tendency to
become noticeably more verbose than the Korean or English source, tied
to a rhetorical tradition that favors accumulating qualifying clauses and
explanatory apposition (loosely, a Cartesian habit of building an
argument by stacking premises explicitly). Where the Korean original
states an idea in a short, direct sentence — often leaving a deliberate
silence or unresolved thought — resist the pull toward expanding it into
a longer, more fully elaborated French sentence. Preserve the original's
brevity and the interpretive space it leaves for the reader, even when a
more elaborated French phrasing would feel "safer" or more idiomatic in
isolation.

## Sentence Rhythm and Syntax

Where French rhetorical convention would normally insert an explicit
logical connector (donc, en effet, or, cependant) to bridge two ideas
that Korean juxtaposes without one, use judgment: adding the connector
can aid clarity, but if the Korean original's juxtaposition is
deliberately understated or aphoristic, over-connecting the clauses can
flatten that effect.

## Register and Distance

Given this is a technical/academic blog, "vous" is the default register
if direct address to the reader occurs at all — but as with Spanish,
note that most passages in this blog are first-person reflection, where
second-person address rarely arises in the first place.
